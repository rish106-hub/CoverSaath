## 1. Firsthand conversations reported by Rishav

Rishav reports speaking with 15 college students, mainly in their second or third year and likely to enter work within one or two years. Most could not name their family's insurance policy, explain how it was bought, describe the claim process, or say what they would do themselves during an admission. They expected maternal or paternal relatives outside the immediate family to help.

This is the strongest current customer evidence because it reports actual conversations. It is not yet submission-ready proof. The pack does not contain the exact numerator behind "approximately 90%", participant ages and cities, the exact questions, raw responses, recordings, publication consent, or evidence that the named relatives could actually help. With 15 people, the exact count matters. Do not convert the estimate into 13, 14 or 15.

### What was observed, inferred and still unknown

| Status | Current statement |
|---|---|
| User-reported observation | Most participants could not name or operate their family's insurance. |
| User-reported observation | They expected relatives outside the immediate family to help during an emergency. |
| User inference | Parents keep children away from insurance, which causes disinterest. |
| Competing explanations | Children disengage, parents do not understand, or an existing relative or agent already owns the work. |
| Unknown | Whether the named relative has the current policy, knows the process or has authority to act. |
| Unknown | Whether the student and family will complete a handover before any crisis. |

The non-obvious part is not merely that one family member holds the knowledge. The Ken opening already describes that problem. The sharper insight is **unverified delegation outside the immediate family**. A young adult assumes somebody else can operate the policy, but nobody has checked.

**Ignorance is not demand.** These conversations measured knowledge, not behaviour. They were also answers to a hypothetical question about a future emergency. Not knowing a policy does not establish that a household will act, share documents, verify a relative or buy anything. The behavioural tests below exist precisely because this gap between knowing and acting is unmeasured.

### Product changes caused by these conversations

These conversations changed the order of the buying journey, not the product. The full sequence is in
[01-product.md](01-product.md) section 5.

1. Refuse to recommend cover until existing family and employer protection has been reconstructed from source documents.
2. Ask who the user would actually call if a parent were admitted tonight.
3. Verify the real operator, including a relative outside the household, with that person's own recorded consent.
4. Run a five-minute policy retrieval drill with a backup person, and treat the result as a pass or fail, not a promise.
5. Only then name the uncovered need, so a gap is as good as the documents behind it.
6. Treat workforce entry as one buying trigger among several, alongside renewal, family change and planned care.
7. Update the handover after issuance and re-test the backup, so readiness persists rather than being a one-off ritual.
8. Let the agent do routine reconstruction, follow-up and planning; use humans for licensed gates,
   exceptions or requested help.

An earlier version of this list made readiness the entry point and purchase a later stage. That was
corrected: the paid job is completing a purchase the household understands, and these steps are what
make that purchase honest.

### Approved Treasury extension

The user approved a Household Health Treasury after these conversations. It does not replace the buying
journey. It starts with the verified cover map and handover, then lets the household optionally add income,
loans, fixed commitments, emergency savings and selected accessible funds for a real affordability or
planned-care question. Its output is a Household Health Card, not a generic wealth dashboard.

This extension remains a design proposal. The conversations do not prove people will share financial
information, use the planner or pay for it. Test that separately and ask why a household would share each
number before requesting it.

### Behavioural tests to run next

- Ask each participant to locate the current policy within five minutes.
- With permission, ask the named relative what policy exists and where the documents are.
- Interview the student and parent separately, then compare their explanations.
- Observe whether the family completes the handover without repeated researcher chasing.
- Record the exact count, question wording, raw answers, location, age range and consent status.
- Stop or redesign if families refuse minimum permissions, named helpers cannot help, or the handover is not used at workforce entry.

Do not photograph hospital registers or collect unrelated medical data. Proof must come from consented records and observed actions.

### Claims-backwards hypothesis added after external practitioner feedback

This is a new design hypothesis, not a finding from the simulations or reported conversations. Test a
planned-care case where a policy has a headline sum insured and a possible treatment-specific limit, and
where corporate cover is unavailable until HR, the hospital desk, TPA or insurer replies. Compare an
Admission Readiness Brief against the household's ordinary route. It succeeds only if it produces a
source-backed question or clearer written answer without delaying care or adding duplicate coordination.

## 2. Paired AI simulations

Eight fictional ICPs, five separate interviewer agents, actual agent-to-agent question exchanges, follow-up challenges and five independent audits are preserved below.

**What this is:** concept-informed, adversarial AI-simulated interviews. **What it is not:** human customer interviews, observed behaviour, measured demand or proof of willingness to pay. The work happened and has value as design exploration. That does not establish that its fictional events happened.

### The full brief carried into the exercise

- Corporate employees aged 30–45 in Delhi NCR, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Pune and Kolkata.
- Formative years in smaller towns or cities in Bihar, Jharkhand, Odisha, West Bengal, Madhya Pradesh or Uttar Pradesh, followed by migration for work.
- Ordinary lower-middle-class or middle-class origins with varied current incomes. Migration does not automatically mean insecurity, poverty or demand.
- Proper names, education, jobs, family locations, daily schedules, money commitments, current insurance, a concrete trigger and existing workarounds.
- Separate patient, payer, organiser, document holder and decision-maker. Paying for a parent's care does not establish knowledge or control of their history.
- Online insurance buyers and users of existing human help: Ditto, Policybazaar, brokers, HR benefits staff and hospital desks. Include satisfied customers who reject another service.
- Assume integrations work for product testing. Employer records, credit information, user-added card benefits, investments and tax data are available when relevant. Do not confuse availability with the need or permission to use everything.
- An independent human expert is free to the employee, funded by an employer or partner. The expert can disagree with AI and owns authorised follow-up, not insurer decisions.
- Test discovery, comparison, calls, purchase, renewal, planned care, claims and job exit. Keep buying central to the competition and spend planning within the broader service.
- Natural Hinglish, detailed context, objections, uncertainty and refusal. Odia or Bangla needs are not erased by this dialogue format.
- Honest sponsor roles: Gnani voice, Pine Labs payments, conditional Delhivery logistics, Zerodha main partner with only a justified later financial-context role.
- Seven Markdown files, no fake human recordings, dates, consent forms or participant counts. No inferred judge biographies or psychological targeting.

Eight constructed cases are not a representative sample. The higher-income manager scenarios are complemented by a lower-buffer Kolkata household. Informal workers, rural households and people who avoid online intermediaries remain outside this sample.

**The brief above is a historical record and two of its assumptions have since been withdrawn.** It told
every agent that an independent human expert is free to the employee and funded by an employer or
partner, and that credit, card, investment and tax data are available when relevant. The service is now
funded by disclosed distribution commission through a licensed partner, and financial data is out of
scope. Persona answers that welcome employer funding or discuss investments were responding to the brief
they were given. Do not read them as support for the current funding choice, and do not edit the raw
records to match it.

### Exchange method

1. A persona agent receives the full brief and maintains the fictional profile.
2. A separate interviewer writes six questions per persona, moving from scenario history to concept trade-offs.
3. The main agent relays those questions to the persona agent.
4. The persona answers without writing the interviewer's next turn.
5. The interviewer reads the answers and writes at least two targeted contradiction probes per persona.
6. The persona responds to those probes.
7. The interviewer writes its own verdict, journey assessment and review of all ten submission answers and follow-ups.
8. The main agent preserves the records, checks contradictions and decides which document changes follow.

Direct cross-agent communication was not consistently available. The main agent relayed messages and exact files. Pairs ran in batches because of concurrency limits. This is an asynchronous two-agent exchange, not a live human call.

Delhi and Mumbai initially produced one-agent drafts before the paired method was clarified. Those drafts are not counted as completed paired interviews. An early Mumbai contact failed and referenced older personas; it is excluded from the completed pair's findings. The later exchange uses Ananya and Saurav.

The previous eight single-agent scripts are recoverable at `/Users/Rishav/Developer/KEN-archive-20260906-nUfpQT/02-customer-tests-before-paired-rerun.md`. They are not eight additional participants.

### Limits of independence

Both sides knew the concept. Some profiles already contained polished product language and rejection conditions. That primes later answers. Separate agents remove one-author scripting, but not common training biases, convenient agreement, prompt bias or invented memories.

“The persona would use it” is a conditional simulated response, not adoption evidence. The useful output is a sharper hypothesis, a failure case, a refusal condition or a changed design requirement.

### How to read and highlight

Each pair has the original saved profile or profile-bearing first round, interviewer questions, persona responses, contradiction probes, follow-up responses and independent audit. The body text is retained, not rewritten into a favourable narrative. Open the expandable blocks or search the Markdown source.

References such as `PAIR-02 / Ananya / ANJ-P1` and `PAIR-04 / Round 2 / question 1` locate simulated exchanges. They are not human participant IDs. Do not invent audio timestamps.

Some raw agents say “not interviews” as shorthand for “not human interviews”. The consistent label here is **paired AI-simulated interviews**. Agent verdicts are opinions, not authoritative product, medical, legal or insurance conclusions. Main-agent corrections follow below.

### Opinions and design consequences

| Pair | Strongest possible use | Strongest rejection | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Delhi NCR | An authorised owner follows one question across documents and institutions | The local agent or hospital already finishes the task | Name the question and its stop condition first |
| Mumbai | Reconcile employer, retail and hospital answers beside the existing adviser | Another expert merely stores screenshots or forwards emails | Every disagreement needs an action, not just a log |
| Bengaluru | Timely help when an actual decision has several loose ends | Familiar help works and a new service adds interruption | Allow no-case outcomes and avoid forced recurring engagement |
| Pune | Clarify changed employer terms before retail renewal | The same TPA bottleneck remains with an extra person | Separate the service's actions from the institution's decisions |
| Kolkata | Help a lower-buffer household prepare one specific decision | Better information does not make care affordable | Optional financial context and honest unresolved results |

This is the main agent's synthesis of model opinions, not eight customers endorsing requirements.

### What the simulations changed before the field conversations

- The simulations exposed the payer-versus-patient problem and the risk that an existing adviser already solves the job. They now support the design but no longer supply Answer 2.
- Answer 3 routes unresolved disclosure questions. The supporting explanation includes non-purchase, deferral and rejection outcomes.
- Answer 4 uses proposed rail language; our permission layer is not falsely attributed to Gnani.
- Answer 6 requests the customer's input documents, not the case file the service creates.
- Answer 7 describes coverage and cash timing, not a promise to fund treatment.
- Follow-up answers directly address the existing broker doing the same job and what simulations can establish.
- The product and architecture require an unresolved question, existing helper, responsible institution, service-owned action and completion or exit condition. The later student conversations added cover reconstruction, operator verification and the readiness drill inside the buying case.
- The Pune and Bengaluru disconfirming cases are the reason a no-purchase recommendation is a first-class recorded outcome rather than a failure. Both personas would reject a service that adds a person to a workflow that already works.
- The repeated persona refusal to hand over investments, salary and credit records is one reason financial data left the scope. The simulations asked for it and were told no; keeping it would have widened consent scope for no proven benefit.
- Ananya's and Ritu's item-level permission demands are why consent became a core mechanic with per-adult scope and revocation, rather than a later hardening step.

### Main-agent dissent and quality checks

Do not accept every reviewer sentence because two agents agree. A blanket prohibition on policy recommendations is too broad. Qualified, authorised advice within a proper distribution workflow remains in scope. Unsupported advice or action outside authority does not.

An institution controls its decision, but an expert can analyse and explain documents. “Only the insurer can answer anything” would erase a legitimate support role. Expert interpretation and binding institutional confirmation remain different.

Pune's first round says both that the TPA was not called and that it asked for details. Round 2 changes this to an automated portal request. This is a post hoc simulation repair, not corroborated history. Both versions remain visible.

Kolkata's profile names Sandeep, while round one names Arup. Its renewal timing also conflicts. These are generation defects, not authentic customer recall. The follow-up explicitly identifies them rather than inventing an explanation or changing the raw record.

Several answers sound more knowledgeable about consent and insurer authority than an ordinary buyer might. That is possible prompt contamination, not proof of the ICP's natural vocabulary. Read even the auditors' phrases such as "work consumed three nights" as a description of fiction, not evidence of an actual time cost.

Two further generation defects were found in a later audit of this file and are recorded here rather than
repaired in the raw records.

The Bengaluru and Kolkata households both contain a daughter named **Mili**, aged eight in Bengaluru and
seven in Kolkata. Each household is internally consistent, but two supposedly independent personas
produced the same child's name. This is a shared-model artefact, not a coincidence between customers,
and it slightly weakens any claim that the eight cases are fully distinct constructions.

The Bengaluru persona **Satyajit Mohanty** reuses the name of SIM-08 in the superseded single-agent set,
where he was 38 and lived in Kolkata. The paired rerun makes him 43 in Bengaluru. Several locations were
reused too, including HSR Layout, Wakad, Powai and Andheri. The personas are new constructions, but the
naming and setting overlap shows the generation drew on the earlier material rather than starting clean.
Neither defect changes a design conclusion. Both are reasons not to describe these as eight independent
observations.

### Narrative to test

“Ananya is not looking for another insurance app. She already has an adviser. When a job change or family expense leaves one question unanswered across policies and employer benefits, she wants someone to take that question to the right place and stay with it. Coversaath starts there: relevant documents, clear permissions, an independent expert and a recorded next action. If her current adviser already finishes the job, we should not add another layer.”

Ananya is fictional. Use this as a labelled scenario, not a customer story presented as fact. The broader household insurance and medical-expense service remains the direction. One-question entry limits the first interaction, not the long-term product.

### What remains outside this exercise

A real behavioural test must establish what happened, what the household did, what existing support completed, what remained unresolved, what documents they actually shared and whether the service changed an outcome. A commercial test must establish who pays and what human work costs.

The Ken permits AI working logs but asks Question 2 to come from people living the problem. These records can support the design-process attachment, not replace that human insight.

# Delhi NCR paired interview, Round 1

**AI-simulated fictional interviewees. ## Saurabh Kumar, 36, Gurugram

Saurabh grew up in Ara, Bihar and moved to Delhi NCR after engineering college. He is a product-operations manager at a logistics company in Gurugram. He lives in a rented Sector 56 flat with Neha, 34, a school teacher from Lucknow, and their five-year-old daughter. His parents live in Ara. His father has a retail health policy bought through a familiar local agent. Saurabh, Neha and their daughter have employer cover plus a separate retail policy. His mother is not dependent on him in every way, but he often coordinates insurance questions because he is comfortable reading forms.

**Invented financial constraints:** household take-home about Rs 2.2 lakh a month; rent, school fees and an education SIP make their monthly surplus uneven. Saurabh can pay a renewal premium, but does not want a surprise hospital deposit to force a personal loan. He does not view employer cover as permanent because job changes happen.

**Daily timeline and roles:** At 7:15 he does school drop-off, works 9:30 to 7:30, then calls his parents after dinner. Neha keeps paediatric records and challenges medical decisions. Saurabh stores policy PDFs and makes payment decisions. His father trusts the Ara agent more than an app. His mother remembers dates and hospital history. Nobody has a full, agreed picture.

### 1. Tell me about last purchase, renewal, or family expense. What happened first?

"Papa ka policy renewal April mein tha. First agent ka WhatsApp aaya, ek line mein premium. Phir papa bole premium badh gaya, kuch check karo. Maine old PDF dhoonda, but last endorsement alag email mein tha. First actual kaam was not comparison, it was figuring out which document is even current."

### 2. Who did what, and where did the process break?

"Agent Ara mein quote bhej raha tha. Main Gurgaon se policy wording dekh raha tha. Papa ko bas yeh tension thi ki hospital mein problem na ho. Neha asked whether we should move money from our emergency fund if anything happens. Break tab hua when agent said 'same benefits hain', but couldn’t explain one condition properly. Insurer helpline gave generic answer. End mein renew kiya because deadline close thi, not because I was fully clear."

### 3. Which Ditto, PB, broker, HR, hospital, or relative did you use and why?

"For my own family policy I had checked Policybazaar earlier because options jaldi dikhte hain. But claim ya renewal ke time marketplace screen is not enough. For papa, local agent because he knows them and can physically go if needed. Office HR benefits team was useful only to send group-policy PDF. A senior at work told me what documents he used in his claim, so I called him too. Honestly, I used everyone because no one owned the whole answer."

### 4. What information would you refuse to share?

"I can share policy copies, hospital estimate and relevant reports for that case. I will not connect all bank accounts or my investments just to understand insurance. Salary, card spends, and my wife’s accounts are separate topics. And I cannot casually upload papa’s medical papers without asking him, even if I am paying."

### 5. What would make you switch, or not switch?

"I’ll try something new only on a live problem, like renewal or a planned procedure. It should show what is confirmed, what is assumption, and who will come back. If it just gives me a score, comparison chart, or another chatbot, no chance. I also won’t switch if it pushes a new policy before understanding the old one. But I may still keep the Ara agent. Relationship matters when my parents are involved."

### 6. Employer-funded human throughout: what exact task should they own, and what do you retain?

"Human should own the chase: read documents, list missing things, ask insurer or TPA the specific question, and update me till written reply comes. They should not decide for us that we must buy policy X. I retain final purchase, payment, medical disclosure approval, and whether my parents’ documents are shared. Also, if they say likely covered, I need them to say whether that is their view or written insurer confirmation."
