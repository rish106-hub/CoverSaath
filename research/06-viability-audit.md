# Coversaath: viability and fit audit

Updated 07 September 2026. Written against the live competition page read in the browser on
07 September 2026, which supplied the ten question prompts and the five judging criteria verbatim.
This file audits whether the idea is right to submit and build. It did not change 01-product.md. The user
approved a direction change later on 07 September; the decision and its guardrails are recorded below.

## 2. Verdict in one paragraph

The opening is the right choice and the insight is the strongest asset in this pack. The wrapper built
around the insight is the wrong thing to submit and probably the wrong thing to build. A
commission-funded broking service with unlimited free human advice and a licensed adviser performing a
separate evidence review on every case is a services business with AI attached. The pack already admits
it has no moat and that Ditto or Policybazaar can copy the sequence. The competition asks for an agent.
The recommendation is to keep the opening, keep the insight, keep purchase as the ending, and move the
centre of the product from the adviser to the agent, with voice as the load-bearing rail and human time
metered rather than unlimited.

### Approved response to this audit

The user approved an agent-led Coversaath with a Household Health Treasury as the long-term record and
planning layer. This accepts the audit's core correction: the agent performs reconstruction, consented
voice intake, source linking, gap assessment, affordability calculations, follow-up and card generation.
Humans are metered at licensed purchase gates, material ambiguity, exceptions or a customer request.

The Treasury does not replace buying as the competition story. It starts after cover reconstruction and
uses voluntary income, fixed commitments, loans, emergency savings and selected accessible funds only
where they answer a specific premium or planned-care question. It must not become a generic wealth app.
Credit reports, full investment holdings and tax data remain later optional sources. The open risks in
sections 4 and 6 remain active: evidence, consent friction, unit economics, licence structure and the
absence of a proven moat.

## 3. What is right and must be kept

- **The opening.** "Buying the insurance" fits the insight, the team's access and the ten questions.
- **The insight.** Insurance knowledge lives in one household member's head, and the backup that person
  names has never been checked. The delegation to a relative outside the immediate family is the
  non-obvious half, and it is missing from the opening's own description.
- **The five-minute drill.** It is falsifiable. It passes or fails and can be reported honestly. This is
  the single most competition-shaped mechanic in the pack.
- **Refusing to recommend before reconstruction.** Counterintuitive and memorable. It should be the
  headline, not a process note.
- **The conflict controls in 01-product.md section 6.** Most teams will not design against their own
  incentive. This scores on Thoroughness and Feasibility.
- **The stop rules and negative cases.** Product Strategy explicitly wants "the wall you hit and why."
- **Track: Product Strategy.** Correct. There is no prototype and three days remain.

## 4. What is wrong and why

### 4.1 The business wrapper contradicts the brief

At the time of this audit, the pack put a licensed human adviser at the centre of every case and described
AI as working backstage. Question 3 asks how the *agent* works. An answer where the human performs the
load-bearing judgement on every case reads as a broking startup with AI garnish, and it invites the
obvious response that Ditto already does this. The approved response in section 2 moves routine work to
the agent and meters human gates.

### 4.2 The economics invert

Free unlimited human support funded by distribution commission means the cases that cost the most earn
the least. An honest no-purchase outcome, which the pack correctly treats as a first-class result, earns
nothing while consuming the most adviser time. A retail family floater premium produces a first-year
commission in the low thousands of rupees. A case that requires reconstructing documents, obtaining
consent from three adults, a call to a parent in another city and a separate adviser evidence review
does not fit inside that. 04-market-and-sources.md already says this may be "enough to kill the model."
The fix is not a higher commission rate. It is a hard budget of human minutes per case, with the agent
doing reconstruction, chasing and tracking, and the adviser entering on exception and at the
recommendation gate only.

### 4.3 The rails story is one rail deep, and not the declared one

| Rail | Current role in the pack | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Payments, Pine Labs | Pay the premium, reconcile, keep commission disclosure separate | Thin. This capability exists today and carries no depth |
| Logistics, Delhivery | "Conditional if physical documents are required and a service is confirmed" | Effectively no role. Say no cleanly with a reason; the page permits this |
| Voice, Gnani | Multilingual parent and relative intake, read-back, expert handoff | The only load-bearing rail, and genuinely strong |

Voice is load-bearing for a concrete reason the pack under-uses: the person holding the knowledge is
often a parent in another city who will not install an app, may not read English policy wording, and
will not type. Reaching that person by voice, under their own recorded permission, is the only path to
the reconstruction step. That is a real dependency, not a decoration. It should be stated as such and
taken deep, because "depth of your load-bearing rail" is judged.

### 4.4 Evidence is the weakest area and it is read first

| Hole | Consequence | Fixable by 10 September |
|---|---|---|
| The 15 conversations have no recorded question wording, raw answers, participant mix or consent | Answer 2 cannot be safely supported or attached | Yes, today |
| No parent or knowledge holder has been interviewed | A submission about household delegation with no conversation with the delegate is the first hole a judge finds. The parent is also the payer | Yes, three conversations |
| No photograph of a real workaround | The page explicitly asks for "the folder of medical reports, the WhatsApp chat someone keeps with themselves." The product is about exactly that folder. Highest value attachment available, roughly one hour of work | Yes |
| No drill has been run with a real family | The design effect claimed in Answer 2 has never been executed once. A failed drill is still publishable evidence | Yes, one household |
| 1755 lines of AI-simulated interviews | Cannot count as customer evidence, and the page says so. Attachable only as working notes or AI chat logs, which the page does allow | Already handled, keep the labelling |

### 4.5 An internal contradiction a judge will catch

Answer 7 annexes planned-admission coordination, which sits inside opening 03, "Managing the family's
health." Answer 8 names "Managing my family's health" as the opening that would never be handed to an
assistant. As written, the submission annexes the thing it says it would not delegate. One of the two
must change. The cleaner fix is Answer 8, because the distinction being drawn there is between
administration and presence, and it should be worded so it cannot be read as refusing our own product.

### 4.6 Hedging costs marks inside the word limits

The caveat discipline in this pack is correct for the research files and should stay. Inside a 30, 40 or
60 word box it reads as absence of conviction. Answer 5 currently ends with "we would test whether Gnani
already supports it," which withdraws the ask in the same sentence that makes it. Assert in the answers.
Keep the limits in the research pack, where Thoroughness is actually assessed.

## 6. Is it a viable product, separate from the competition

Viable as a mechanic inside a distribution or benefits platform. Not yet viable as a standalone company,
for four reasons that are already visible in this pack.

1. **Cost per case against income per case.** Unbounded human help paid for by first-year commission on
   a low-premium retail product does not close. Metered human time is the only version worth modelling.
2. **The consent chain is the funnel.** Value appears only after the buyer, the current operator and
   sometimes a parent elsewhere all cooperate. Each consent is a drop-off. This is a harder cold start
   than a comparison site.
3. **No moat, stated by the pack itself.** The order of steps is copyable. The possible advantage, a
   provider-neutral household record with a verified delegation graph and a longitudinal disclosure
   history, only compounds for whoever owns the buying relationship over years. That is a bet.
4. **Regulatory work is unfinished and cannot finish this week.** Distribution licence route, the advice
   boundary, and health data under multi-adult consent all need qualified review. State the exposure in
   the submission; do not assert a conclusion.

None of these blocks the submission. Product Strategy asks for the wall and the reason. These are the
wall, and naming them precisely scores better than hiding them.

## 7. Recommended direction

Keep the opening, the insight, the drill and the ten-step buying sequence. Move the centre of gravity
from the adviser to the agent:

- Headline the refusal. The agent will not recommend cover until a second named person in the household
  can find and use the existing policy. Signature mechanic, stated in one line.
- Voice becomes the declared load-bearing and innovation rail, with the parent-in-another-city
  dependency spelled out.
- Human time becomes metered: agent reconstructs, chases and tracks; the licensed adviser reviews once
  at the recommendation gate and on exception. This fixes the economics and matches the brief.
- Purchase completion, issuance check and updated handover stay as the ending, so the submission still
  answers "Buying the insurance" rather than drifting into a readiness ritual.
- Move every caveat out of the ten answers and into the research files.

## 9. Claims-backwards update, 15 September

Two practitioner comments received after this audit sharpen the continuity layer without changing the
opening. The important correction is that a policy's sum insured is not a procedure-level promise:
therapy, condition and room rules can materially constrain a planned admission, and corporate-plan terms
may not be visible to an employee until a hospital event. The architecture should therefore produce an
Admission Readiness Brief with cited limits, unanswered questions, document needs and named institutional
owners across hospital desk, HR, TPA and insurer.

This is not evidence of demand, prevalence or a claims-API gap. A claims-first strategy would be fragile
because Coversaath cannot assume insurer or TPA system access. Keep buying as the opening and test the
brief manually against one real or fully synthetic planned-care case. Stop if it merely restates a policy
PDF or adds a slower layer than the existing hospital or TPA route.
