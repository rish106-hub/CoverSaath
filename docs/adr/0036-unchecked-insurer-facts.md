# ADR 0036: Show model-extracted insurer facts as "not checked"

- Status: Proposed (2026-11-14). Owner decision taken; implementation follows the first extraction run. Not reviewed. Builds on ADR 0034 (FSS as the insurer tier). FOR LEGAL REVIEW: the display wording.

**Context.** FSS will extract insurer facts from public policy wordings with a model (FSS `docs/contracts/extraction-v1.md`, signed off 2026-11-14). Every such fact is `extracted_unverified`, with a page and verbatim quote. CoverSaath today drops any insurer fact that is not `independently_verified`: `parseFact` blanks its value, and `fss-fact-map.js` maps only `outcome === 'verified'`. Migration 049's pairing CHECK also allows `ida_wording` only with `reviewed_reference | conflicting | unknown`. So none of the extracted facts would ever reach a user. On 2026-11-14 the owner chose to show them, labelled "From the insurer's wording, not checked".

**Decision.**
1. **Status.** `ida_wording` gains `extracted_unverified`. `TIER_STATUSES.ida_wording` becomes `reviewed_reference | extracted_unverified | conflicting | unknown`. A new migration replaces `household_memory_facts_tier_status_check` the same way (expand-only, existing rows untouched).
2. **Quote end to end.** Today the quote is dropped on the way in: `parseEvidence` keeps no quote, and `fss-fact-map.js` and `fss-reference-facts.js` set `quote: null`. This ADR adds the quote along the whole path. FSS `versions.facts` evidence carries `quote` (verbatim, at most 200 characters, from extraction-v1). `parseEvidence` parses it (`optionalText`, bounded at 200, control characters stripped). The reference evidence that `fss-fact-map` seals into `reference_ref_enc` keeps it, and the display reads it back from the sealed reference only.
3. **Parsing.** `parseFact` (integrations/insurance-data/schemas.js) keeps the value of a non-synthetic fact whose `verification_status` is `extracted_unverified` **only when at least one *parsed* evidence item has a page and a non-empty quote**, with `outcome: 'unchecked'`. The gate is on the parsed evidence, never on the raw array. Everything else is unchanged: `verified` still needs `independently_verified` plus family coverage. The value is bounded by the same parsers.
4. **Mapping.** `fss-fact-map.js` maps `outcome: 'unchecked'` to an `ida_wording` row with status `extracted_unverified`, and `verified` to `reviewed_reference` as today. The evidence (page, quote) is sealed as now.
5. **Collate.** An `ida_wording` row with `extracted_unverified` is never the holding's decided value. `collate.js` keeps today's order: `reviewed_reference` insurer > own PDF > unknown. An unchecked insurer value only fills a key that has no own-PDF value, and it keeps the status `extracted_unverified`. It is never promoted.
6. **Never a verdict.** The existing status filters stay and are named here so this change cannot widen rule inputs: chat drops every `status === 'extracted_unverified'` fact before procedure planning (`chat-v1-service.js`, the `relevant` filter for `procedure_planning` and the facts passed to `planProcedure`), whatever its tier. Rules (`insurance-rules`) and computed checks read only `reviewed_reference` and confirmed values. Pin tests: an `ida_wording` `extracted_unverified` row never reaches `planProcedure`, a computed check or a rule result. In chat, an unchecked insurer fact may be cited as a source (answer card `sources[].tier = 'ida_wording'`, `status = 'extracted_unverified'`), and the guard's checks apply. The card `verdict` stays `unknown`.
7. **Display.** The policy card and chat show "From the insurer's wording, not checked", with the page and the quote. The label is chosen from **tier and status together**, never status alone: today both `chat-answer.js` (`SOURCE[fact.status]`) and the model slot label in `chat-v1-service.js` would call an unchecked insurer fact "read from your policy document". Both get a `(sourceTier, status)` lookup with tests. The beta tile badge maps to `ida_wording` with an "unchecked" variant. Wording FOR LEGAL REVIEW.
8. **Erasure and retention.** The household rows (`household_memory_facts`, `ida_wording`, with the sealed evidence and quote) are erased with the holding, the document or a revocation, as now. `reference_cache` is public insurer data with no household, case or consent key (migration 033), so household erasure does not and cannot touch it. It keeps its own policy: entries expire by `stale_after` (import TTL, default 30 days), are replaced by the next import, and are removed by the `import-` rollback. No new store.

**Consequences.** Users see insurer terms for products where the policy itself is silent. That's the value of the extraction run. The risk is a wrong extracted value shown as insurer wording, contained by: the label, the quote shown with it, the extraction gate (≤ 2% false values on the golden set), the never-a-verdict rule, and the guard. A later independent review (FSS checker workflow) promotes a fact to `reviewed_reference` with no CoverSaath change.

**Alternatives rejected.**
- Keep dropping unverified insurer facts until reviewed: safe but slow, and nothing reaches users for months (owner rejected).
- Treat extracted facts as `reviewed_reference`: breaks the rule that a model never writes `reviewed_reference` (AI.md, migration 049).

**Implementation touchpoints** (one PR, after the first extraction run): FSS exporter `versions.facts` evidence `quote` · `src/contracts/policy-facts.js` (`TIER_STATUSES`) · new migration (pairing CHECK) · `src/integrations/insurance-data/schemas.js` (`parseEvidence` quote, `parseFact` gate on parsed evidence) · `src/features/policy-facts/fss-fact-map.js` and `src/backend/services/fss-reference-facts.js` (carry the quote in the sealed reference) · `src/features/insurance-rules/collate.js` · `src/features/insurance-rules/chat-answer.js` and `chat-v1-service.js` (label by tier and status) · beta tile badge (Knowvia `src/lib/beta/tiles.ts`) · tests: quote required, raw-column, household erasure, never-a-verdict pins (planning, checks, rules), labels, migration, policy-facts registry drift. Needs an independent read-only review (AI.md: migration, AI and trust change).
