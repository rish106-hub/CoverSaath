## Current position

**North Star.** No family should have to understand its health insurance for the first time during a
medical crisis.

**USP.** Coversaath creates a living, source-backed understanding of a household's insurance, then turns it
into the next clear action across purchase, renewal, hospitalisation and claims. It does four things
together:

- Explains what the household has.
- Separates confirmed facts from assumptions.
- Identifies what could create problems.
- Coordinates the people and institutions needed to resolve them.

It does not predict whether a claim will pass. It produces a **claim-readiness assessment** showing
supporting evidence, risk signals and unanswered questions.

### What the research established

Eight consented interviews, 5 to 8 September 2026, transcribed in full. Respondents were 26, 27, 29, 31,
33, 34, 55 and 58. Two parents. One hospital insurance-desk executive with six years at the desk and two at
a TPA, interviewed in person, who declined payment.

The number that anchors the product: **7 of 19 collected documents had a schedule field that did not match
the family's own account of it.** One sister's date of birth was six years wrong on a live policy schedule.

Directional, not a prevalence study. Respondents came through friends, family and their acquaintances.
Every one of them is privately insured; nobody is on a state scheme. Stated wherever these findings appear.

The reported opening share is 3.92%, the third-lowest share of submissions. That is a competition signal,
not customer evidence.

## 1. What is the outcome your agent is accountable for?

> **Coversaath is accountable for a household understanding its health insurance, resolving the
> uncertainties that matter, and knowing what to do next — from purchase and renewal through
> hospitalisation and claim coordination.**

For each case, the deliverable is a person-specific, source-linked route: what existing cover may apply,
what does not yet apply, what must be confirmed, and what cash may be needed by which deadline.

Completion is testable:

- No material question disappears into a summary.
- Every important statement links to evidence or is labelled as reported from memory.
- The household can identify its next action, owner and deadline from the brief.
- Group cover, personal cover, available cash, loans and investments remain separate.
- A shared floater, deductible, waiting period, room rule, co-pay, top-up and fixed-benefit payment are
  modelled as conditions, not added into one reassuring number.
- In an emergency, administrative analysis never delays treatment.

**Why this outcome and not "saves money" or "gets claims approved".** Arnab lost ₹80,000 because he picked
a room ₹1,200 above his cap and nobody told him a room-rent breach triggers a proportionate deduction
across the whole bill [T1 00:06:15]. Meghna was three months into buying a 20L top-up with a 10L deductible
sitting on an 8L base — a ₹2 lakh dead zone she found live on the call [T4 00:01:43]. Neither failure is a
price failure. Both are understanding failures, and both happened before anyone reached a hospital.

The accountable outcome is not claim approval. Coversaath cannot bind an insurer, decide underwriting,
select treatment, guarantee cashless approval or promise settlement. It is accountable for reducing
avoidable uncertainty and unfinished household work while preserving institutional authority.

**The output rule, taken verbatim from a respondent.** Faizan: "Everyone gives me the counterfactual. I want
the next step." [T7 00:05:41]. Every screen will be tempted to explain what the household should have done.
None of them is allowed to stop there.

## 2. What level of autonomy does your agent have?

> **L3: acts independently within household-approved limits.**

After the named adults set the purpose, permissions and communication limits, Coversaath can act without
asking again inside those limits. It can:

- Read policies, schedules, endorsements and benefit documents.
- Reconstruct the household's available cover, per person.
- Identify missing or conflicting information.
- Contact an already approved family member.
- Prepare and send factual questions to insurers, TPAs, HR teams and hospitals.
- Track replies, deadlines and documents.
- Compare replies with policy wording and earlier correspondence.
- Update the household's insurance record and prepare the next decision brief.
- Check citations, source versions, conflicts and unanswered material questions before release.

It must ask before:

- A new person sees protected information.
- A new institution is contacted outside the agreed purpose.
- Sending a medical declaration or proposal answer.
- Accepting a licensed recommendation.
- Paying a premium.
- Filing a claim.
- Accepting a settlement.
- Any borrowing or investment action.

**It is not L4** because insurers, TPAs, hospitals and licensed advisers still control the outcomes that
matter. An adviser cannot bind an insurer. Family relationship never creates authority over another adult's
records.

**The evidence constrains autonomy in a specific direction.** Mrs. Ghosh will let a stranger on an emergency
line see everything, because she tells her doctor — but she will not let her own son see the reason behind
a condition on her declaration [T5/T8 00:03:06]. She granted an emergency override in advance, for herself,
knowingly [T8 00:02:16]. An agent that treats "the family" as one permission boundary fails her. Autonomy is
bounded per field and per viewer, not per household.

## 3. What states does your agent go through?

### Happy flow

```text
REAL EVENT          Purchase, renewal, job change or planned treatment
      |
PERMISSION          Who owns the information and who may access it
      |
RECONSTRUCTION      Policies, employer benefits, endorsements, medical disclosures
      |
UNDERSTANDING       What exists, what it may cover, what remains unknown
      |
DECISION            Retain, renew, change, buy, or prepare for treatment
      |
CONFIRMATION        Specific questions to the insurer, TPA, HR team or hospital
      |
ACTION              Approved declarations, payments, documents, follow-ups
      |
TRACKING            Issuance, pre-authorisation, claim status, deadlines
      |
RECONCILIATION      Record what actually happened; improve the next decision
```

Every state carries four fields: **current owner, evidence used, unresolved question, deadline.** Evidence
is marked confirmed by document, confirmed by institution, reported by household, Coversaath assessment,
unknown, or conflicting. **Unknown never means absent.**

That three-state discipline is not a design preference. It survived contact with the documents: 7 of 19
schedules disagreed with the family's own account. If the model cannot hold "the document and the person
disagree" as a first-class state, it will print a confident wrong answer.

**At purchase or renewal.** The agent compares current cover with the household's needs and may recommend
retaining, buying additional cover, changing cover, or taking no action at all. Payment happens only after
approval. The case stays incomplete until issuance and endorsements are verified.

**Before a planned admission**, it produces an **Admission Readiness Brief**: relevant policies;
applicable-looking benefits; waiting periods, exclusions and limits; what remains unconfirmed; documents
likely required; cashless and pre-authorisation questions; possible hospital deposit and cash scenarios;
who must complete each action; deadlines and escalation routes.

Two requirements in that brief came directly from the hospital desk and from a claim that stalled. Vikas
says families arrive empty-handed and then hunt for an ATM, so the brief must state the **upfront cash
number** [T2 00:03:42]. Sourav's claim sat for two months because nobody told him a road-accident claim
needs an FIR copy, so the document list must be **specific to the case type**, not generic [T5 00:07:17].

### Unhappy flow

| Situation | Agent response |
|---|---|
| Treatment is an emergency | **Admit first, optimise later.** Connect the authorised human to the smallest safe live view. Administrative work resumes only when it cannot delay care |
| Permission is missing | Stop. Request permission from the relevant adult. Hide that adult's data and halt dependent tasks |
| Policy document is missing | Do not infer that cover is absent. Contact the approved holder and mark the policy unknown |
| Documents conflict | Preserve both versions, dates and source pages. Request institutional clarification |
| Family memory conflicts with policy wording | Treat the document as evidence and memory as unconfirmed. Surface the conflict; do not silently pick one |
| Voice transcript is uncertain | Do not convert uncertain speech into a declaration. Read back, confirm, switch language or channel |
| Insurer or TPA does not reply | Follow up, record attempts, show the approaching deadline, escalate through a permitted route |
| Hospital network status is unclear | Do not describe the hospital as cashless |
| Pre-authorisation is delayed | Show deposit and reimbursement scenarios |
| Pre-authorisation is denied | Escalate. Do not call it a final claim rejection |
| A rail or API fails | Preserve the case and evidence state. Fall back to a permissioned call, WhatsApp, email or manual route |
| Premium is paid but policy is not issued | Keep the purchase unresolved. Reconcile until issued, reversed or disputed |
| A claim appears weak or excluded | Do not invent an approval probability or coach concealment. Explain the condition, the missing evidence and the responsible institution |
| The existing adviser already solves the job | Avoid duplicate onboarding. Work alongside with permission, or close the case |
| User asks for medical advice | Route to the treating doctor |
| A serious source or privacy error is found | Block release and outbound action. Notify the owner, revoke affected access, correct the record, require review |

## 4. What does it use and build on each rail?

### Gnani: voice

**What exists.** Multilingual speech, configurable voice agents, knowledge bases, dynamic pre-call
variables, external actions, conversation logs and analytics.

**How Coversaath uses it.** Voice is how the product reaches the person who holds the document and the
permission but will not upload or type. The agent explains a question in the person's preferred language,
collects missing information and reads the answer back for correction.

This is not a convenience feature. S. Ghosh, 58, holds the family policy and cannot get past the insurer
login [T3]. Mrs. Ghosh's entire 19-minute session ran in Bengali, on a phone, at her request [T8]. Neither
of them is going to open an app. Contacting a dependent or backup family member is a secondary use, not the
main product.

**What Coversaath must build.** A permission-aware insurance conversation layer that records who supplied
each fact, what document supports it, who may see it, whether it was confirmed or disputed, and which
unanswered question must escalate.

**Documented ceilings we must design inside.** Maximum three languages per agent — and this research already
used English, Hindi and Bengali, so the cap binds on day one. Maximum 100 FAQ entries. Agent Chaining is
bot to bot; there is **no documented bot-to-human warm transfer**. No multi-party or conference call.
Post-call webhook only, with no mid-call event stream, so a supervising system cannot intervene live.

**It must never** treat spoken recall as proof of coverage, or share one adult's medical information with
another without permission.

**Fallback.** A scoped human call, or a WhatsApp questionnaire. Material voice facts stay unconfirmed until
the speaker approves them.

### Pine Labs: payments and authorisation

**What exists.** Payment links, hosted checkout, payment status, refunds and reconciliation. The public
documentation at `api.pluralpay.in` also describes card **pre-authorisation with hold and capture**
(`pre_auth: true`, then capture or cancel), **UPI Reserve Pay** block-and-debit, split settlement, and
agent-native payment flows.

**How Coversaath uses it.** It collects a premium after the household approves a purchase or renewal and
sees the commission disclosure. The more interesting use is the hold: a hospital deposit can be authorised
and released rather than paid and chased, which is exactly the moment Vikas describes when families arrive
without cash [T2 00:03:42].

**What Coversaath must build.** An insurance transaction record connecting recommendation → quote and policy
version → medical declarations → household approval → premium payment → insurer issuance → endorsements →
final policy record. For a hold: payer-to-patient authority, amount ceiling, expiry, release condition,
cancellation deadline, idempotency and reconciliation.

**Honest gaps.** **eNACH and NACH are not named in Pine Labs documentation; we do not claim them.** No
hold-expiry window is documented, and whether a hospital can be an eligible merchant accepting an authorised
hold is unverified. A payment hold is not insurance pre-authorisation, and it is not a right to use another
adult's money.

**It must never** present payment success as proof that the policy has been issued or that a future claim
will be covered.

**Fallback.** The licensed partner's approved payment route or the hospital's own route, reconciled manually.
No payment link appears before the recommendation and disclosure gates.

### Delhivery: logistics and maps

**What exists.** Shipment creation, pickup and tracking. Delhivery Maps provides Indian address geocoding,
standardisation, validation, verification, routing and distance tools.

**How Coversaath uses it.** Only after a hospital or insurance office has been institutionally confirmed,
validate its address and help the family plan the route. Physical delivery is used only if original
documents are genuinely required.

**What Coversaath must build.** A small layer connecting the confirmed institution to the correct branch,
the insurance-desk location, contact person, operating hours, document checklist and travel time. If real
originals move, add a consent token, tamper-evident chain of custody, exact-recipient proof and a return or
destruction status.

**It must never** select the nearest hospital and assume it supports cashless treatment under the user's
policy. Address validation does not verify identity or insurance eligibility.

**Delhivery is useful but not load-bearing.** There is no point inventing a parcel workflow to feature the
rail, and we are saying so rather than padding the answer.

### What is load-bearing

Not a rail. The permissioned, source-linked cover record is load-bearing; the rails are how it reaches
people, money and places. Gnani is the **reach** rail, Pine Labs the **event** rail, Delhivery the
**last-mile** rail.

Documentation checked 20 September 2026: [Gnani Agent Builder](https://docs.gnani.ai/introduction),
[Gnani Platform API](https://docs.gnani.ai/Platform/platform-introduction),
[Pine Labs hosted checkout](https://www.pinelabs.com/docs/online-payments/hosted-checkout/integration-steps),
[Delhivery B2C APIs](https://one.delhivery.com/developer-portal/documents/b2c/),
[Delhivery Maps](https://www.delhivery.com/maps/reference). The Gnani console has not been tested for this
submission; console-only behaviour remains unverified.

## 5. Does the agent need a fourth rail?

> **Yes. An Insurance Confirmation Rail, and Medi Assist is the company best placed to build it.**

Today, policy explanations, hospital information, insurer emails, TPA replies and pre-authorisation updates
live in different systems. Coversaath can organise them. It cannot make them authoritative.

The rail should return dated, case-specific information:

- Active policy and member status.
- Latest endorsement.
- Confirmed TPA.
- Hospital network status.
- Required documents.
- Pre-authorisation status.
- Claim status.
- Pending institutional action.
- Responsible team and escalation route.

**Why Medi Assist.** It already sits between insurers, hospitals and policyholders and operates
claims-administration workflows. It is the only kind of party that could return a *dated* answer rather than
an inference.

**Why the rail is needed, from the evidence.** Every failure in this study is a confirmation failure, not an
information failure. Nikhil had a 17-day portability window and no notification when the cover lapsed —
"nothing happens, that's the thing" [T6 00:01:39]; his mother is uninsured at 62 nine months later. Sourav
lost seven hours at 2 a.m. because an employer reception wanted an employee ID he did not have
[T5 00:03:08]. Vikas, from the desk, is explicit that he cannot file a claim off a third-party sheet, though
he would use one to convince a family [T2 00:03:38]. A rail that returns status changes all three outcomes.
A better summary changes none of them.

**The hard part inside this rail is delegated household authority.** The rail must be able to answer not
only "what is the status" but "is this person allowed to ask". That means a signed, revocable authority
record: the principal; the delegated person or agent; the permitted action (read, ask, share, pay, receive);
the exact records, institution, purpose, amount limit and expiry; live revocation; an audit trail; and a
denial state when authority is absent, expired, disputed or broader than the task.

Setu's Account Aggregator documentation is the closest existing Indian pattern — purpose-specific consent
objects, approval or rejection, expiry and status. It is a useful operational starting point and worth
citing as prior art. It does **not** establish a health-data, family-delegation or third-party action API.

**This rail confirms status. It does not guarantee approval.** It must not turn a family relationship, a
payment token or a delivery address into authority, and it must not expose a claim-approval probability.
A claim score without an insurer-validated model creates false confidence.

Without this rail, Coversaath falls back to per-institution consent, customer documents and manual
follow-up, and any missing answer stays marked unknown.

## 6. How will a human interact with the agent?

The interface has three parts, and they are deliberately not equivalent.

### WhatsApp — the main working interface

This is where the household already is. The user receives one question at a time, missing-document
requests, plain explanations, approval requests, institutional updates, deadline reminders and the next
recommended action.

WhatsApp is **not one of The Ken's three rails**, and we are saying that plainly rather than pretending
otherwise. It is the surface. The rails are how the product reaches past the surface.

Why it is the main interface and not voice: the person who signs up is a working adult with a phone, and a
threaded text record is the only format where a household can go back and check what was said. Meghna's
decisive moment was reading a citation — "it says here, page fourteen" [T4 00:06:06] — and she said she
could not win an argument with a relative-agent using "an app told me". A citation has to be re-readable.
Voice cannot hold a source line still.

### Voice, on Gnani — the reach rail

The user, or the household, can ask Gnani to explain a policy term, collect missing facts, read back
recorded information, contact an approved family member, or walk through an admission checklist.

Voice exists because **the person who signs up and the person who holds the documents are usually different
people**. S. Ghosh, 58, holds the family policy and cannot get through the insurer login [T3]. Mrs. Ghosh
gave nineteen minutes in Bengali on a phone call she asked for [T8]. WhatsApp does not reach either of them
for the thing we actually need from them.

### Voice and WhatsApp, side by side

Both are needed, and the answer is not "one of them wins". They cover different failures.

| | WhatsApp does this better | Gnani voice does this better |
|---|---|---|
| **Who it reaches** | The adult who signs up and runs the case | The parent, relative or knowledge holder who will not type or upload |
| **Language** | Written, re-readable, any script | Spoken, code-switched Hindi, Bengali, English as the person actually talks |
| **Citations** | A source line stays on screen and can be shown to a relative [T4 00:06:06] | Cannot hold a source still; must read back and confirm |
| **Documents** | Upload, forward a renewal PDF, attach a schedule | Cannot carry a document; can only describe one |
| **Effort at 2 a.m.** | Still requires opening an app. Sourav: "I will not open an app" [T5 00:04:22] | One call, no screens, no policy-number interrogation [T5 00:04:36] |
| **Someone who cannot use a smartphone well** | Fails | Works |
| **Audit trail** | Native. Every message is the record | Needs a transcript, a read-back and an explicit confirmation before a fact is trusted |
| **Ceiling** | Template and session-window rules on business messaging | Three languages per agent; 100 FAQ entries; no warm bot-to-human transfer; post-call webhook only |

The design rule that falls out: **WhatsApp carries the case; voice carries the people the case depends on.**
A fact collected by voice is marked *reported*, read back, and only becomes *confirmed* when the speaker
approves it or a document supports it. Neither channel becomes a second uncontrolled record — both write
into the same case.

### Case page

The user sees one event-based case, not a large insurance dashboard. The first question is: **what is
happening now?** Buy or renew. Job or family change. Planned admission. Claim problem. Emergency access.

The case shows five blocks:

- **What we know** — source-backed facts, per person.
- **What may matter** — limits, conditions and claim-risk signals.
- **What is unknown** — questions awaiting confirmation, with an owner and a deadline.
- **What cash may be needed** — deposit and payment scenarios, stated as a number.
- **What happens next** — action, owner, deadline.

Every important statement carries a label: confirmed by document, confirmed by institution, reported by
household, Coversaath assessment, unknown, or conflicting. A user can correct any extracted fact and see
its source. A correction does not erase the earlier record.

Four working modes sit inside the case: the **per-person view** (people on one axis, group and personal
policies on the other; never added into one guaranteed number); **plan an expense**; **buy or renew** (which
can output retain, defer or "find out X first"); and **emergency access**.

**Emergency access is specified by a respondent, not by us.** Sourav: one button is the maximum, two screens
is already too many [T5 00:04:22], and the person who answers must not ask him anything — "I'll only say my
brother's accident, I'm at [hospital] — the rest they should already know" [T5 00:04:36]. So the
specification is not "a human". It is a human who does not ask for a policy number.

**Permissions are per-field and per-viewer**, with a member-approved emergency override, because
Mrs. Ghosh's rule is "the number yes, the reason no" [T4/T8 00:02:52]. Her son may see that cover exists and
how much, because that keeps him calm. He may not see the declaration that explains why a condition is
listed. She consented to a full emergency override in advance, for a moment when she could not speak
[T8 00:02:16].

A licensed human appears as a marked gate, not a permanent concierge. They receive disputed facts and their
sources, record their reasoning, and return the case to the household.

## 7. What is the name of the agent?

> **Coversaath**

Primary descriptor: **Understand your insurance before you need it.**

Product line: **Know what you have. Know what could go wrong. Know what to do next.**

*Saath* means "with" or "alongside" — the product sits beside a household rather than selling to it, which
is the same promise as being allowed to say "not this, and not now". Name, trademark, domain and company
clearance remain unverified.

## 8. Which Indian company has the best chance of creating an agent like this?

> **Policybazaar.**

Policybazaar already operates across insurance comparison, purchase, renewal, policy servicing and claim
assistance. It has the widest existing position from which to build a similar agent: shoppers, licensed
distribution, insurer relationships, payment flows and post-purchase support. Its own claim-assistance page
states that the insurer makes the final settlement or rejection decision, so the boundary is already
acknowledged.

**The gap is not access or technology.** The difficult part is building an agent that treats these as
equally valid outcomes: buy, renew, retain the existing cover, defer the decision, and "we cannot confirm
this yet". A conversion-led business may find that last one hard to prioritise, because it is the only
output that produces no transaction. This is an external inference about incentives, not knowledge of
Policybazaar's internal plans.

Coversaath's differentiation must therefore be the **persistent household record and its discipline around
unknowns**, not comparison, policy explanation or claim assistance. Meghna's outcome is the test: the right
answer for her was to spend nothing and redirect the money to her parents [T4 00:05:37]. If Policybazaar
builds the same pre-sale household record with credible conflict controls, most of the remaining
differentiation disappears.
